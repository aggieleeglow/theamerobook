Academic & Policy Research

North American Monetary Union — Economic Research Database

This research database collects academic, policy, regulatory, and technological references related to North American monetary integration, stablecoin regulation, digital currency infrastructure, XRP cross-border payments, and the future of money. It is designed to help readers study the broader context behind The Amero: Digital Bridge to the Fintech Future.

Disclaimer: This database is provided for educational and research purposes. Inclusion of a source does not mean endorsement of every claim or conclusion. Some items are academic or official sources, while others are book thesis references, market commentary, or speculative context. Readers should evaluate each source carefully.

Herbert Grubel — The Case for the Amero

1999 · Academic
Academic

Herbert Grubel's foundational proposal for a North American monetary union, published through the Fraser Institute. This paper introduced the concept of the Amero as a shared currency for Canada, the United States, and Mexico.

Why it matters: This is the origin point for serious academic discussion of a North American currency. It provides the intellectual foundation that The Amero: Digital Bridge to the Fintech Future builds upon and updates for the digital age.

Herbert Grubel — The Case for the Amero (PDF)

1999 · Academic
Academic

The full PDF version of Grubel's original proposal, hosted by the Economic Freedom of the World project. Provides the complete text of the foundational argument for North American monetary integration.

Why it matters: Direct access to the primary source document allows researchers and readers to evaluate the original arguments on their own terms.

Open Source

Thomas Courchene and Richard Harris — From Fixing to Monetary Union

1999 · Academic
Academic

A C.D. Howe Institute paper examining the spectrum of exchange rate arrangements, from fixed rates to full monetary union, in the North American context. Analyzes the economic conditions under which monetary integration might be considered.

Why it matters: Provides a rigorous economic framework for evaluating the feasibility and implications of North American monetary coordination, complementing Grubel's political proposal.

Open Source

Robert Mundell — Optimal Currency Area Theory

1961 · Academic Foundation
Link to be verified

Robert Mundell's seminal work on optimal currency areas, which established the theoretical framework for evaluating when nations should share a common currency. This Nobel Prize-winning research underpins all subsequent discussions of monetary union.

Why it matters: Optimal currency area theory is the economic foundation for evaluating whether North American monetary integration is feasible. Without understanding Mundell's criteria, the policy discussion lacks analytical rigor.

Robert Pastor — Toward a North American Community

2001 · Policy Research
Link to be verified

Robert Pastor's influential work on North American integration, examining the political, economic, and institutional conditions for deeper cooperation between Canada, the United States, and Mexico. Addresses both monetary and non-monetary dimensions of regional integration.

Why it matters: Pastor's work provides the political science complement to the economic arguments for North American integration, offering a comprehensive vision that extends beyond currency to institutions and governance.

ECB — The Euro, A New Currency

1999 · Official
Official

An early speech and publication from the European Central Bank documenting the launch of the euro and the principles guiding European monetary unification. Provides a first-person institutional perspective on the creation of a shared currency.

Why it matters: The euro remains the most significant real-world example of multinational monetary union. Understanding its origins is essential for any serious analysis of whether a similar project could work in North America.

Open Source

European Commission — History of Euro

Ongoing · Official
Official

The European Commission's official resource on the history and purpose of the euro, documenting the political and economic evolution of European monetary integration from its origins to the present day.

Why it matters: Provides the institutional narrative of how monetary union was achieved in practice, including the political compromises and governance structures that made it possible.

Open Source

CFR — The Eurozone in Crisis

2015 · Policy Research
Academic

A Council on Foreign Relations backgrounder analyzing the structural weaknesses exposed by the eurozone sovereign debt crisis, including fiscal policy divergence, banking sector vulnerabilities, and the limitations of monetary union without fiscal union.

Why it matters: Any proposal for North American monetary integration must contend with the lessons of the eurozone crisis. This source provides a clear analysis of what went wrong and why.

Open Source

ESMA — MiCA Regulation

2023–2024 · Official
Official

The European Securities and Markets Authority's overview of the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA), the EU's comprehensive regulatory framework for digital assets, stablecoins, and crypto-asset service providers.

Why it matters: MiCA represents the most advanced regulatory framework for digital assets globally. It provides a benchmark against which U.S. and North American regulatory approaches can be compared.

Open Source

ECB — Digital Euro Project

2021–2026 · Official
Official

The European Central Bank's ongoing project to develop a digital euro, a central bank digital currency (CBDC) for the eurozone. Documents the investigation phase, design considerations, and policy implications of a digital form of central bank money.

Why it matters: The digital euro project demonstrates how an existing monetary union is adapting to the digital age, offering direct parallels and lessons for any proposed digital currency framework in North America.

Open Source

U.S. Treasury — GENIUS Act

2026 · Official
Official

The U.S. Treasury's official statement on the GENIUS Act (Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins), landmark legislation establishing a federal regulatory framework for payment stablecoins in the United States.

Why it matters: The GENIUS Act represents the most significant U.S. regulatory development for stablecoins. It defines the legal environment in which digital payment infrastructure will operate across North America.

The presidential executive order on strengthening American leadership in digital financial technology, establishing policy direction for digital assets, stablecoins, and blockchain innovation in the United States.

Why it matters: This executive order signals the highest level of U.S. government engagement with digital financial infrastructure and sets the policy context for subsequent legislation and regulation.

CLARITY Act Status

2026 · Current Policy
Market

Coverage of the CLARITY Act's progress through the U.S. Senate Banking Committee, legislation aimed at establishing regulatory clarity for digital assets and distinguishing between securities and commodities in the crypto space.

Why it matters: The CLARITY Act, alongside the GENIUS Act, represents a pivotal moment in U.S. digital asset regulation. Its progress signals how Congress is approaching the broader question of digital financial infrastructure.

U.S. SEC — Ripple Enforcement Action

2020 · Official
Official

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission's press release on its enforcement action against Ripple Labs, alleging unregistered securities offerings related to XRP. This case became one of the most closely watched regulatory proceedings in the digital asset space.

Why it matters: The SEC v. Ripple case defined the regulatory landscape for XRP and influenced broader debates about how digital assets should be classified and regulated in the United States.
