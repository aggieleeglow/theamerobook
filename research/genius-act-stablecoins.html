The GENIUS Act & the Stablecoin Foundation

The Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins (GENIUS) Act, signed into law in July 2025, is the first comprehensive federal stablecoin framework in American history. It also happens to be the regulatory scaffolding a Digital Amero needs to exist.

The market the law was written for

Total stablecoins outstanding~$275 billion (mid-2025)
Annual transaction volume~$15 trillion
USD-denominated share~99% of all currency-backed stablecoins
Tether + Circle T-bill holdings$120B+ in short-dated US Treasuries

Two things to note about that table. First: the asset class is now too large to ignore. Second: it has quietly become one of the largest non-foreign holders of US Treasury bills, which means stablecoin policy is now fiscal policy.

What the GENIUS Act requires

  • Full 1:1 reserves in cash or short-dated US Treasury bills. No commercial paper, no algorithmic backing.
  • Federal or state charter for issuers. Unregulated issuance is now unlawful at scale.
  • Monthly attestation, annual audit by a registered public accounting firm. Reserves are public information.
  • Bankruptcy priority for stablecoin holders — they are senior to other creditors of the issuer.
  • Anti-money laundering obligations equal to those of insured depository institutions.
  • Foreign-issued stablecoin restrictions if the home jurisdiction does not maintain comparable standards.

Why this matters for the amero

The 1999 Grubel proposal needed a treaty: three countries had to agree to abolish their currencies and accept a new tri-national central bank. The political ask was enormous, which is why the proposal stalled.

The 2025 digital amero needs no such treaty. It needs three regulatory regimes — one in each country — that all permit fully-reserved, audited, fiat-backed stablecoins. The GENIUS Act delivers the US side. Canada's regulatory work at the OSFI and the Bank of Canada (notably the 2024 retail-CBDC consultations) and Mexico's CNBV stablecoin guidance fill in the rest. A continental settlement layer becomes a matter of mutual recognition, not constitutional change.

The Trump executive order context

On January 23, 2025, President Trump signed an executive order that prohibits a Federal Reserve retail CBDC and explicitly directs the federal government to promote private USD-denominated stablecoins worldwide. The strategic logic is sometimes called "cryptomercantilism": project dollar dominance through private dollar tokens rather than through a state CBDC. The GENIUS Act is the legislative complement.

For North America, this matters in a counter-intuitive way. A US administration that refuses a Federal CBDC removes the most likely competing architecture for a continental settlement layer. The lane the GENIUS Act opens is exactly the lane a digital amero proposes to occupy.

"When the United States chose private stablecoins over a Fed CBDC, it did not close the door on a continental currency. It built the door." — Lee, The Amero: Digital Bridge to the Fintech Future, ch. 9

Key dates

  • Jan 23, 2025 — Trump executive order prohibits Fed CBDC, mandates pro-stablecoin posture
  • July 2025 — GENIUS Act signed into law
  • Q4 2025–2026 — Federal rulemaking and chartering procedures finalized
  • 2026 onward — First wave of GENIUS-chartered issuers comes online; mutual-recognition discussions with Canada and Mexico begin
