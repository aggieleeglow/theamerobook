About the Author

Che-Sun Lee

Blockchain strategist, social entrepreneur, and the author of The Amero: Digital Bridge to the Fintech Future — the 2025 book that reframes North American monetary integration for the era of programmable money.

Background

Che-Sun Lee is a Toronto-based author and policy advisor whose work sits at the intersection of monetary economics, blockchain infrastructure, and continental policy. She holds a B.Tech from Toronto Metropolitan University and has advised governments, financial institutions, and technology firms across North America and Asia on digital-asset strategy, cross-border settlement, and stablecoin regulation.

Her debut book, The Amero: Digital Bridge to the Fintech Future, was published in 2025 and runs to 33 chapters and approximately 75,000 words. It is, by design, the first comprehensive treatment of the amero proposal that integrates Herbert Grubel's foundational 1999 academic argument with the technical, regulatory, and geopolitical realities of 2025: the GENIUS Act, the XRP ledger, the digital euro project, and de-dollarization.

What the work argues

The thesis is direct: a shared digital settlement layer across the United States, Canada, and Mexico — the Digital Amero — is the smallest political ask that delivers the largest economic gain. Each country keeps its currency. Cross-border friction does not. The book is read alongside academic work on optimum currency areas, modern stablecoin policy, and the architecture of public blockchains.

It is a book for policymakers, business leaders, fintech engineers, students, and citizens. It is not a book for conspiracy theorists. The opening chapters take particular care to distinguish the academic record from the NESARA / GESARA narrative the term "amero" is sometimes confused with online.
